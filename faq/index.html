Frequently Asked Questions

Last updated November 13, 2026

Questions about the Build-Signs.work network as a whole. Each sign builder also has its own FAQ for its kind of sign, linked from the bottom of every page there.

What is Build-Signs.work?

Build-Signs.work is a network of nine free sign builders, each for a different kind of sign. They share one sign service that draws every sign from scratch, so a road sign, a trail sign and an airfield sign all come out as clean, standards-based artwork you can download as a PNG, an SVG or a printable PDF.

Which site should I use for my sign?

Pick the one for where the sign would stand:

  • Road Signs: STOP and speed limit signs, street-name blades, Interstate shields, warning diamonds and big green guide signs, to the federal MUTCD.
  • Trail Signs: trail junctions, blazes, difficulty markers, forest-road shields and wilderness signs, to the Forest Service Sign and Poster Guidelines.
  • Park Signs: brown-and-white park entrances, campground and attraction signs, campsite markers and trailheads.
  • Airport Signs: FAA runway holding position, taxiway location, direction and destination signs and sign arrays.
  • Safety Signs: OSHA and ANSI Z535 DANGER, WARNING and CAUTION signs, HazCom labels, NFPA 704 diamonds and ISO 7010 signs, in ten languages.
  • Railroad Signs: crossbucks, advance warning signs and the rest of MUTCD Part 8, plus station signs and model-railroad signs printed to scale.
  • ADA Signs: room, restroom and exit sign layouts with Grade 2 braille to the 2010 ADA Standards, with STL files for 3D printing.
  • Boating Signs: waterway markers, dive flags, marina and dock signs, beach flags, boat registration numbers and boat names.
  • DOT Signs: USDOT number lettering, hazmat placards and labels, oversize-load banners and highway signs for truckers.

Is it really free?

Yes. There's no account, no watermark and no paid tier, and every download is the full-quality file. The sites pay for their servers with ads and a small commission on printed products from Zazzle. You never need either to download a sign.

Can I use the signs commercially?

Yes. Every sign image the builders make carries the Creative Commons Public Domain Mark: use it, change it, sell it, with no credit needed. Public domain covers copyright only, so trademarks you type onto a sign are still their owners', and laws about making a sign look official still apply. The Licenses page has the details, including every font and artwork source.

Are the sites connected to the FHWA, FAA, OSHA or another agency?

No. The sites are an independent project, not government sites, and aren't affiliated with or endorsed by any agency, standards body or company. The designs follow public standards (the MUTCD, the Forest Service sign guidelines, FAA advisory circulars, OSHA and ANSI Z535, the 2010 ADA Standards, 33 CFR and 49 CFR), but a download is a picture of a sign, not a certified sign.

Can I put up a sign I made?

As decoration, on your own property, almost always. On a public road, a trail, an airfield, a waterway or in a workplace, a real sign has to meet its standard, be made of the right materials and be approved or installed by the authority responsible. Each site's FAQ explains the rules for its kind of sign, and the Terms of Service cover what the sites can and can't promise.

How are the signs made?

Each builder is code that lays a sign out from its standard: the sizes, borders, colors, letter heights and spacing are worked out for the words you type, and checked side by side against the official drawings. Fonts are public-domain digitizations of the FHWA highway alphabets and other public-domain faces. No sign is a traced photo or a copy of a stock image.

Were the sites built with AI?

Yes, with a lot of help. The code, the sign builders and much of the written content were written with the help of Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. AI can make mistakes, and so can people, so check anything that matters against the official standard. See Built with AI in the Terms of Service.

Do the sites track me?

Only a little, and never by name. The sites keep pseudonymous, aggregate analytics of which signs are built, use Google Analytics and show Google AdSense ads, and sell nothing. There are no accounts. The Privacy Policy explains it all, including how to turn off personalized ads.
