About Build-Signs.work

Last updated November 13, 2026

Build-Signs.work is a family of free tools for making signs that look like the real thing. Each of its nine builders covers one kind of sign and the standard behind it. Type your own words or numbers, watch the sign redraw as you go, and download it as a PNG, an SVG or a printable PDF. There’s no account, no watermark and no paid tier.

The builders

  • Road Signs: STOP and speed limit signs, street-name blades, Interstate shields, warning diamonds and big green guide signs, to the federal MUTCD.
  • Trail Signs: trail junctions, blazes, difficulty markers, forest-road shields and wilderness signs, to the Forest Service Sign and Poster Guidelines.
  • Park Signs: brown-and-white park entrances, campground and attraction signs, campsite markers and trailheads.
  • Airport Signs: FAA runway holding position, taxiway location, direction and destination signs and sign arrays.
  • Safety Signs: OSHA and ANSI Z535 DANGER, WARNING and CAUTION signs, HazCom labels, NFPA 704 diamonds and ISO 7010 signs, in ten languages.
  • Railroad Signs: crossbucks, advance warning signs and the rest of MUTCD Part 8, plus station signs and model-railroad signs printed to scale.
  • ADA Signs: room, restroom and exit sign layouts with Grade 2 braille to the 2010 ADA Standards, with STL files for 3D printing.
  • Boating Signs: waterway markers, dive flags, marina and dock signs, beach flags, boat registration numbers and boat names.
  • DOT Signs: USDOT number lettering, hazmat placards and labels, oversize-load banners and highway signs for truckers.

One sign service behind all of them

The nine sites share one sign service. It draws every sign from scratch as vector artwork, laying out its border, colors, letter heights and spacing from the published standard for that kind of sign, and the builders are checked side by side against the official drawings. Because the code is shared, a fix or an improvement made for one site often reaches the others too.

The fonts are public-domain digitizations of the FHWA highway alphabets and other public-domain faces, and the pictographs are either U.S. government artwork or drawn for the project. Agency emblems, trademarks and protected insignia are never drawn.

Free, and public domain

The software itself is licensed under the BSD 3-Clause License. The Licenses page has the full details, and the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy cover every site in the network.

What a sign from here is, and isn’t

A download is a picture of a sign: great for a garage wall, a classroom, a model layout, a film set, a training slide or a plan for a real sign. It isn’t a certified traffic-control device, airfield sign, safety sign or tactile ADA sign. Those need the right materials, approval and installation from the authority responsible.

Who runs it

Build-Signs.work is an independent project, not a government site, and isn’t affiliated with any agency or standards body. It was built with the help of Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, and is paid for by ads and a small commission on printed products from Zazzle. The FAQ answers the questions people ask most.
